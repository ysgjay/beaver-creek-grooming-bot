      How the forecast is built

      1. Raw models. NOAA's GFS, HRRR and National Blend, ECMWF, Germany's ICON, Canada's GEM and the UK Met Office model, pulled for this exact point with temperature corrected to 10,476 ft. The NWS forecasters' gridded snowfall joins as one more voice.
      2. Our own snow ratio. Liquid precipitation becomes snow using a temperature-based ratio tuned for dry Colorado snow (about 15:1 in the teens) instead of the flat 7:1 most free apps use.
      3. Bias correction. Each model is checked every day against the Vail Mountain SNOTEL gauge. A model that runs dry here gets scaled up; one that keeps missing gets less weight.
      4. Weighted blend and range. High-resolution models lead for the next 48 hours, global models after that. 82 ensemble runs supply the likely range and powder odds.

      This is the same recipe OpenSnow describes for its PEAKS model (downscale public models, then bias-correct against stations), built from free public data.
